Principles

Small software can be thoughtful, capable, and fair without becoming complicated. These are the decisions that guide what we build — and what we deliberately leave out.

  • Focused by design

    Each product starts with a clear job. Features should make that job better, not simply make the feature list longer.

  • Fair pricing

    Pricing should reflect the product and its real costs. We prefer straightforward purchases where they make sense and avoid recurring fees that exist mainly to support a business model rather than the product.

  • Local where it makes sense

    If something can work well on the device, there is often no reason to put a server in the middle. Local technology can make software faster, more resilient, more private, and less expensive to operate.

  • Technology with restraint

    New technology is useful when it solves a real problem. We do not add AI, cloud services, accounts, or infrastructure simply to make a product sound more advanced.

  • Honest interfaces

    People should understand what an action does, what something costs, and what happens to their data. Design should help people make decisions, not steer them through tricks.

  • Users stay in control

    Settings, assistance, notifications, and monetization should support the experience rather than take it over. The product should remain a tool for the person using it.

  • Data minimization

    Collecting less is often the simplest privacy feature. When external services or data processing are necessary, we aim to be explicit about what they are for.

  • Small is allowed to stay small

    Not every product needs to become a platform. A well-made application with a clear purpose can simply keep doing that job well.
